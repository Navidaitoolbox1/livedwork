# Why lived experience is the last advantage

*By Navid Taheri*

## Machines learn faster than we do

I trained as an architect. I spent years learning how buildings stand up, how light moves through a room, how a plan becomes a site. For seven years I ran my own architecture office and taught at an architecture faculty at the same time.

Today a model can explain most of what I learned in those years in a few seconds, in clear language, in any language.

That is not a complaint. It is the new ground we all stand on. **Anything that depends only on knowledge or technical expertise is becoming cheap.** If your value is "I know how this works," you are now competing with something that knows more, works for free, and never sleeps.

So the real question is not how to compete with AI. It is: what is left that only you can bring?

## What is left

When I look at my own path, the things that moved my work forward were rarely the things I knew. They were the things I had lived.

In 2021 I moved to France and had to build a professional network from zero, in a language I was still learning. No model can do that for you. It is soft skill and stubbornness: walking into rooms, being misunderstood, trying again.

At 42 I started an MBA in marketing at a French business school, because I had learned something the hard way: if you don't understand business, positioning and distribution, talent stays invisible. Architecture gave me form. Business gave me structure. AI gives me scale. But the connection between the three exists only in my own history.

This term I teach students in French. Explaining a concept in your second language is hard. It is also something a model can't do in my place: the students are not only learning the content, they are watching how a person who struggled with the same things thinks.

That is the pattern. **Knowledge is the material. Lived experience is the point of view that decides what to do with it.**

## What to focus on

If knowledge is getting cheap, invest where it isn't:

- **Lived experience.** Write down what happened to you. Moving countries, changing careers, failing, rebuilding. These are not side stories. They are your raw material.
- **Soft skills.** Teaching, negotiating, listening, building trust. They take years and they transfer to everything.
- **Judgment and taste.** When everyone can produce, the person who chooses well becomes valuable.
- **A visible point of view.** Your view is worth nothing while it stays in your head. It needs to be captured, shaped and published.

LivedWork is built around exactly these four.

## How this turned into income for me

I'm not going to promise anyone a million-dollar launch. Here is what actually happened in my case.

**Writing came first.** I write in Persian on X about AI, strategy and work. Over a few months, those pieces were read more than 30 million times. I didn't build an audience by posting news. I built it by explaining things through my own experience: architecture, migration, building products alone.

**The audience's questions became products.** My French book on working with Claude sold 490 copies in its first month, with no advertising at all. Across my Persian, English and French books, more than 1,280 people have now read my work. The book did not need ads because the topic had already been tested in public, one post at a time.

**Community before product.** My architecture podcast, Archgap, did not start as a product. It started during lockdown as Instagram lives with fellow architects. People gathered first. The podcast came after: 42 episodes and around 17,000 subscribers.

**Building is cheap now, being seen is not.** In the past year I built Decoran, an AI interior design app, with a team of specialists, and RezoCV, a job application assistant, entirely on my own. With today's tools, building was not the hard part. The hard part was always the same: getting it in front of the right person, with a reason to trust it.

The lesson I keep relearning is the one LivedWork is built on: **a first product works when people have already asked for it, and you have already solved the problem yourself.** I call it Signal + Solved.
