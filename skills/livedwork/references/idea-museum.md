# Idea Museum

A place to keep every idea before it disappears. Marketers call it a swipe file. We call it a museum because what you keep there shapes how you see.

## The four rooms

| Room | What goes in | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Loved** | Ideas you personally find exciting, from books, talks, conversations | A line from a book you can't stop thinking about |
| **Proven** | Topics and posts that clearly worked for others in your space: unusually high replies, saves, shares | A post in your niche with ten times the usual replies |
| **Lived** | Moments from your own life and work that taught you something | The day a client said no, and what you changed after |
| **Questions** | Questions people ask you, in comments, DMs, meetings, at dinner | "How did you find your first job abroad?" |

The best content usually sits where rooms meet:
- **Lived + Proven** = a topic people already care about, told through something only you experienced.
- **Loved + Questions** = an idea you enjoy that answers what people actually ask.

Questions is the most important room for business. Repeated questions are free market research.

## Daily capture ritual (under 5 minutes)

1. Capture the moment it appears. Voice note, phone note, screenshot. Don't edit.
2. Once a day, give each new item a room and one line: why did this catch my attention?
3. If it connects to a past item, link them.

## Weekly review (15 minutes)

1. Read the week's items.
2. Mark three that pair across rooms.
3. For each, write one sentence: what do I believe about this that most people don't?
4. Move them to "Ready to develop".

## Where good ideas come from

Most people consume the same news and the same trends, so they produce the same posts. Look for **high-density sources**: older or less known books, essays where almost every sentence is worth underlining, and a short list of creators who always say something worth thinking about. Keep that list small and visit it on purpose, instead of scrolling the full feed.

And the densest source of all is your own life. Most people never add it to the museum because it feels too ordinary. It isn't.
