# The thinking behind LivedWork

Written by Navid Taheri, architect, MBA student in marketing and CRM, and founder of Navid AI Toolbox.

## Knowledge is getting cheap

Machines now learn faster than we do. A model can read more books in an afternoon than a person reads in a lifetime, and it can explain most of what is in them clearly and politely.

So anything whose value depends only on knowledge or technical expertise is getting cheaper every month. Not worthless, but cheaper. If your work is "I know how X works", a model will soon know it too, and it will answer at 3 a.m. for free.

The useful question is not "how do I compete with AI on knowledge?" You won't. The useful question is: **what do I have that a model cannot generate for someone else?**

## What stays valuable

1. **Lived experience.** What happened to you, in your body, in your city, in your career. The client who almost left. The country you moved to. The exam you failed and retook. A model can describe experiences in general. It cannot have yours.

2. **Judgment.** Choosing what matters, what to ignore, and what to say no to. When production is free, choosing becomes the expensive part.

3. **Soft skills.** Teaching, listening, negotiating, calming a room, building trust with a stranger, giving hard feedback kindly. These are learned through years of contact with real people.

4. **A point of view.** The particular way your experiences connect. Two people can read the same book and leave with different conclusions. That difference is the only thing nobody can copy.

5. **Trust.** People follow people they recognise. Someone one or two steps ahead of them, with a similar life, is often more useful than a celebrity expert.

## Why most people can't see their own advantage

Everyone has a point of view. Most people can't see it because it isn't written anywhere. Ideas vanish within a day if they are not captured, and when you sit down to write, the only thing you can see clearly is whatever is trending. So you write what everyone else writes.

LivedWork fixes this in three steps: capture ideas so they don't disappear (Idea Museum), make your point of view visible (Signature Interview), and turn it into a body of work and a first product (30-Day Sprint).

## Influencer, personal brand, craftsman

This framing is borrowed from Dan Koe's essay on building a body of work.

- An **influencer** optimises for attention.
- A **personal brand** optimises for conversion.
- A **craftsman** builds a body of work, one idea and one project at a time, and lets social media pay for it.

LivedWork is a tool for the craftsman. Attention and income matter, but they should be side effects of doing good work for the right people.

## The role of AI in this skill

AI is the interviewer, the organiser and the critic. It is not the author. It asks the questions you would not ask yourself, sorts what you capture, and tells you when a line sounds like everyone else. The key sentences stay yours.
